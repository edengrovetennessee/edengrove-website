The Development

What Witherspoon Is

01

The Setting

Witherspoon runs south of Crockett Road in Brentwood, between the Somerset and Raintree Forest subdivisions and directly against Crockett Park. It was laid out across 263 acres of the Holt family’s land — part of the historic Wildwood estate, held by the same family for around two centuries. The c.1840 Holt house, Holtland, was placed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988 and was preserved in the plan, along with 7.45 acres around it.

City of Brentwood development plan, Spring 2016; National Register of Historic Places; Williamson Source, 2015–2017

02

The Community

153 homesites, developed by CPS Land with Ford Custom Classic Homes. Sales opened in January 2017 and the last houses were finished in 2025. Every lot sold to a private buyer carried one condition: the house had to be built by a member of the Witherspoon Builders’ Guild — eleven vetted builders including Castle Homes, Barlow, Turnberry, Legend and Ford. That single rule is why the streetscape holds together. Witherspoon hosted the 2017 Parade of Homes.

CPS Land; developer release, April 2019; Home Builders Association of Middle Tennessee

03

The Homes

American, English, French and Italian-influenced architecture on lots running from roughly half an acre to three acres, the largest wooded and on the ridge. Houses sold in the past year ran from 4,745 to 13,928 square feet, most commonly five bedrooms, with four-car garages typical at the upper end. Build years span 2017 to 2025, so the stock is uniformly modern — there is no older housing to renovate here.

CPS Land; RealTracs MLS via nashvillehome.guru and granthammond.com, 2026

Against Brentwood

Across the whole of 37027, 269 homes closed above $2M in the same period at a $2,754,358 median and $480 per square foot. Witherspoon sits above both, on median price and on price per foot, and ranked fourth among Brentwood subdivisions by luxury transaction volume.

What Moves the Price

Square footage matters less than the year on the plaque. The two highest prices per foot in the set — $710 and $612 — were both 2025 builds, while the largest 2018 house returned $420. A newer house here carries a premium of roughly 50% per foot over the first wave of construction.

The Record

9330 Joslin Ct closed at $8,521,458 in September 2025 — 13,928 square feet at $612 a foot, and 101% of asking. It remains the highest price ever paid in the community.

Availability

Getting In

There Is No New Inventory

Witherspoon is finished. All 153 homesites were sold and built out by 2025, and the developer’s own listing shows the community as sold out. Every purchase from here is a resale, which makes timing the whole game.

Roughly Nine Houses in a Hundred

Fourteen of the 153 homes changed hands in the past year — a little under one in ten. Over three years, 45 sales closed at a 28-day median time on market. Stock is scarce but it is not stagnant.

Six on the Market Now

Asking prices run $2,600,000 to $6,750,000, at a $4,174,950 median. That is a wider spread than the closed market, and the gap between ask and close is where the work is.

Shared Spaces

Amenities

Clubhouse and Pools

A clubhouse with kitchen and gathering space, an adult pool and a separate children’s pool, a pavilion and a neighbourhood green for events.

Three Miles of Trails

Roughly three miles of paved and natural walking and cycling paths inside the community, connecting directly to Crockett Park, Crockett Elementary and Woodland Middle. A ten-foot public multi-use path was required by the City and forms part of Brentwood’s trail network.

The Entrance

A pond and a European-inspired roundabout at the entry off Crockett Road, with pocket parks, retained tree rows and the original spring house kept in the landscape.

Homeowners’ dues are charged by section — recorded at $245 a month in Section 1 and $808 a quarter elsewhere, covering grounds, recreation facilities and common areas. Confirm the figure for any specific address.

Nearby

Things to Do

Crockett Park

164 acres directly against the community — ten miles of paved trails, the Eddy Arnold Amphitheater, tennis courts and the c.1830 Cool Springs House. About a mile.

Marcella Vivrette Smith Park

400 acres with six miles of hiking and a mountain bike trail system opened in 2024, wrapped around Ravenswood Mansion, built 1821–25. About two miles.

Radnor Lake

Tennessee’s first State Natural Area — 1,400 acres around an 85-acre lake, with 7.75 miles of trail and no pets or jogging on the Lake Trail. About nine miles.
